The Dogon Sirius claim has been presented in numerous books and documentaries as proof of ancient alien contact: a people in West Africa who possessed detailed knowledge of a star invisible to the naked eye, knowledge that Western astronomy only confirmed with telescopes. If true, this would be remarkable.

The problem is that the evidentiary foundation for the claim does not hold up to scrutiny. The follow-up research is damaging. And the most credible explanation for the claimed knowledge is not ancient aliens — it is contamination through prior Western contact.

## The Original Claim

The claim derives from fieldwork conducted by anthropologists Marcel Griaule and Germaine Dieterlen, who spent extended time among the Dogon people and published accounts of Dogon cosmological knowledge. They reported that the Dogon possessed detailed knowledge of Sirius B — an invisible white dwarf companion star to Sirius — including its approximate orbital period, its density, and its status as the "heaviest star."

Sirius B had been discovered by Western astronomers using telescopes well before Griaule and Dieterlen's research. The question raised by their findings: how could the Dogon have known?

## The Tier System Applied

**Tier 1 — Documented fact:** Griaule and Dieterlen published accounts of Dogon cosmological knowledge that included apparent knowledge of Sirius B's existence and properties. Sirius B had been confirmed by Western astronomy well before this research. The Dogon had documented prior contact with Western missionaries, travelers, and researchers, including French astronomers and scientists, before Griaule's extended fieldwork. Walter van Beek and a research team subsequently conducted fieldwork with the Dogon specifically to investigate the Sirius knowledge claims, and could not replicate Griaule's findings.

**Tier 2 — Suggested by the evidence:** The most credible conventional explanation for any genuine Dogon knowledge of Sirius B is contamination — the Dogon had prior contact with Westerners who knew about Sirius B, and some of that knowledge could have entered Dogon discourse before being recorded as traditional knowledge.

**Tier 3 — Speculative:** That the Dogon possessed genuinely ancient indigenous astronomical knowledge of Sirius B that predates Western contact. That this knowledge was transmitted by alien contact in the distant past. These claims are possible in principle but are directly challenged by the failed replication and the documented prior contact timeline.

## What Van Beek Found

Walter van Beek conducted extensive fieldwork with the Dogon specifically to investigate the Sirius knowledge claims. He and his research team found that the contemporary Dogon they worked with described Sirius differently from Griaule's accounts. When asked about the star, informants did not mention a companion. The detailed knowledge of Sirius B's orbital period and density that Griaule had described was not evident.

Some informants, van Beek reported, indicated that knowledge of a companion star came from Griaule himself — that the anthropologist had taught them about it. This finding, if accurate, suggests that Griaule's methodology may have introduced the very knowledge he believed he was discovering.

Van Beek was careful not to dismiss Griaule as a fabricator. He attributed the problem to methodological issues common in long-term, deeply immersive fieldwork — the anthropologist's framework shaping the informants' responses over years of collaboration.

## The Honest Assessment

The intellectually honest position on the Dogon Sirius case is that the evidentiary foundation for the claim is seriously damaged. The original research rests on a single source. That source's methodology has been credibly questioned. Follow-up research failed to replicate the key findings. The prior-contact timeline makes contamination a straightforward explanation.

This does not prove the claim is false. But the evidence does not support it at the level required to call it established.
