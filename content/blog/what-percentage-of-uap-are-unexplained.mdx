The majority of UAP reports have explanations. Weather balloons, commercial drones, birds, optical illusions, sensor glitch artifacts, misidentified aircraft seen from unusual angles — the greatest share of sightings that reach investigators are assigned to a known category.

But official investigations have consistently found that a meaningful minority of cases resist explanation even after thorough analysis with full access to classified sensor baselines. That unexplained residual — not the total count — is what drives the scientific and security interest.

## What "Unexplained" Actually Means in Practice

In official UAP investigation, a case classified as unresolved has a specific technical meaning. It does not mean briefly examined and dismissed.

A genuinely unresolved case has typically been analyzed against classified databases of known aircraft signatures, sensor calibration profiles, atmospheric phenomena records, and known foreign technology capabilities — and still doesn't fit any known category. The people conducting that analysis have more relevant information than outside observers. When they cannot explain something, that finding carries weight.

## The Tier System Applied

**Tier 1 — Documented fact:** Official investigations have consistently found that the majority of reported UAP cases have conventional explanations. Those same official bodies have also consistently acknowledged that a residual of cases does not. This acknowledgment comes from the investigators with the most complete access to relevant classified information — not from fringe researchers.

**Tier 2 — Strongly suggested, unproven:** The characteristics reported in the unexplained residual — objects with no apparent propulsion, anomalous acceleration, apparent ability to transition across air and water — go beyond what sensor artifacts would predict. But instrument limitations, data quality, and observer reliability all introduce genuine uncertainty.

**Tier 3 — Speculative:** That the unexplained residual represents non-human technology or intelligence. This is the most widely-discussed interpretation of the residual. It is not confirmed.

## Why the Residual Matters More Than the Percentage

The instinct is to say: most UAP have explanations, so the rest probably do too — we just haven't figured them out. This sounds like reasonable inference. But it assumes the unexplained residual is a harder-to-identify version of the same mundane cases, rather than a genuinely different category.

The most credible unexplained cases share specific features that distinguish them from explanations-pending: simultaneous corroboration across independent sensor platforms, performance characteristics that don't map to known engineering at any classification level, and review by analysts who have already eliminated every conventional explanation available to them.

The residual isn't just "we haven't figured it out yet." In the most credible cases, it is "every explanation we have access to has been tried, and none fit."

## What the Residual Doesn't Prove

Official acknowledgment of unexplained cases is not a confirmation of extraterrestrial origin. "We cannot explain it" is a statement about the limits of current analysis. It is a genuinely interesting finding. It is not a confirmation of what the objects are.

The gap between "officially unresolved" and "confirmed non-human" is the tier gap between Tier 1 and Tier 3. That gap exists in the evidence regardless of how confidently either side asserts its position.
