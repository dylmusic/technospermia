Skilled Egyptian workers built the pyramids. We know who they were, roughly how many of them worked, how they were organized, where they lived, and how they were cared for when injured. We know some of this because archaeologists excavated their village. We know more because one administrator left a diary describing moving limestone blocks to the construction site — the oldest administrative papyrus ever discovered.

The alien hypothesis is not only unsupported by evidence. It is unnecessary. The real story is more impressive.

## The Worker Village at Giza

Excavations below the sands near the pyramids revealed a permanent settlement — the workers' town. It contained bakeries capable of producing large quantities of bread, breweries, storage facilities for grain and meat, and medical facilities where workers received treatment for injuries. The remains show evidence of healed fractures — bones that broke and were set and treated with enough success that the workers continued laboring for years afterward.

The burial ground associated with the site contained workers interred with care and marked with small tombs — an honor in ancient Egypt inconsistent with slave status. Workers buried with reverence were not chattel. They were members of a state labor project that cared sufficiently about their contribution to mark their graves.

## The Tier System Applied

**Tier 1 — Documented fact:** A workers' village has been excavated at Giza containing bakeries, breweries, medical facilities, and a burial ground where workers were interred with care. The Wadi al-Jarf papyri — the oldest administrative papyri ever discovered — describe limestone transport logistics for the Giza construction. Workers were organized into named labor gangs. Graffiti in relief chambers reference these gangs by name. The workforce is estimated by modern archaeologists at roughly twenty thousand workers for the largest projects, not millions.

**Tier 2 — Largely understood, some open questions:** The specific engineering methods for lifting and placing the heaviest granite elements remain subjects of scholarly discussion. Multiple credible ramp and lever theories have been proposed and partially tested. No single method is universally accepted, but none requires technology beyond known ancient Egyptian capabilities.

**Tier 3 — Speculative:** That alien or supernatural assistance was involved. That a lost advanced civilization provided the knowledge. These claims contradict the documented evidence of a human workforce using documented tools and logistics, and they add nothing to the explanatory challenge that is not already addressed by archaeology.

## Why the Human Story Is More Impressive

The alien hypothesis, ironically, diminishes the pyramids. It converts an extraordinary human achievement into a demonstration of someone else's technology. It asks us to believe that the same people who produced some of the world's greatest sculpture, medicine, writing, and administrative sophistication somehow couldn't build a large stone structure without outside help.

The documented reality is better: people without steel, without cranes, without computer modeling, without any of what we consider modern infrastructure, organized tens of thousands of workers over decades and built monuments that have stood for millennia. They left records of how they did it. Their administrators wrote diaries. Their workers were buried with honor.

That is a human story worth being amazed by.
