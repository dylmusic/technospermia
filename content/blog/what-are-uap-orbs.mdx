Orb-shaped objects dominate credible UAP reports. Military pilots describe them. Declassified sensor footage captures them. Congressional members who have been briefed on classified UAP material have referenced orbs specifically in public statements about what they were shown.

The consistency of the morphology — a featureless sphere or near-sphere, often metallic or reflective in appearance, with no visible propulsion system or flight surfaces — across independent accounts and sensor platforms is one of the more striking patterns in the data.

## What the Reports Describe

A typical orb-type UAP account involves an object that is roughly spherical, without wings, exhaust, rotors, or any surface structure associated with flight. The objects are often reported at varying altitudes, sometimes in close proximity to military aircraft, and in some cases described as transitioning between air and water without the disturbance that would accompany a known airborne vehicle entering water.

These are not rare or fringe accounts. The orb description appears in formal military incident reports, in testimony at congressional hearings, and in statements from officials describing classified footage.

## The Tier System Applied

**Tier 1 — Documented fact:** Orb-type objects appear consistently across independent UAP accounts from credible sources with relevant professional access. Members of Congress have referenced orbs in public statements following classified briefings. Declassified footage includes objects with spherical or near-spherical morphology. The morphological consistency is a documented pattern in official case records.

**Tier 2 — Strongly suggested, unproven:** The performance characteristics attributed to orb-type objects in the most credible accounts — rapid acceleration, no acoustic signature, air-to-water transitions — go beyond the performance envelope of known human aerospace engineering at any available scale.

**Tier 3 — Speculative:** Non-human technology. Probe-type devices of extraterrestrial origin. Any specific interpretation of what the objects are.

## Why the Consistency Matters

In a dataset with many noise sources — misidentifications, hoaxes, sensor errors, atmospheric effects — consistent morphological patterns across independent accounts from unconnected observers using different sensor platforms are a signal worth attention.

If orbs were primarily sensor artifacts, the artifact pattern would vary predictably by sensor type. If they were primarily drones, the size, performance, and altitude profiles would match known drone capabilities. The cases that survive rigorous screening share specific features — including the orb morphology — that don't map cleanly onto any single conventional explanation.

That consistency doesn't confirm non-human origin. It does make the mundane-only explanation harder to maintain with the most credible cases.
