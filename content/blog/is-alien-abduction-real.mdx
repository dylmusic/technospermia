Alien abduction experiences are real. Thousands of people report them, many are psychologically healthy by clinical standards, and the accounts share striking features across cultures and contexts. The experiences — as experiences — are documented, genuine, and not fabricated.

Whether they represent literal extraterrestrial contact is a completely different question. The honest answer: probably not in most cases, but the phenomenon is more interesting than a simple dismissal suggests.

## What Experiencers Actually Report

The typical abduction account includes: sudden paralysis, a perceived presence in the room, beings appearing at the bedside or aboard a craft, medical or reproductive procedures, non-verbal communication, and a period of missing time. Strong emotional residue — often awe mixed with fear — persists afterward.

These features appear across accounts from people who have never communicated with each other, in different countries, across generations.

That cross-cultural consistency is the thing that makes this worth taking seriously, even while the mainstream explanations hold.

## The Tier System Applied

**Tier 1 — Documented fact:** Abduction experiences occur. They are documented in clinical literature. Sleep paralysis is a real neurological phenomenon — paralysis on waking or falling asleep, hallucinated presence, intense fear — with well-established features that overlap substantially with abduction accounts. John Mack, a Pulitzer-winning Harvard psychiatrist, ran a formal research program and found no evidence of pathology in most of his subjects. Harvard investigated him for professional conduct. He was cleared.

**Tier 2 — Strongly suggested, unproven:** The cross-cultural consistency of abduction phenomenology goes beyond what random hallucination would predict. The overlap between abduction accounts and high-dose DMT experiences — the beings, the medical context, the sense of being studied, non-verbal communication — is striking and documented in research settings.

**Tier 3 — Speculative:** Literal extraterrestrial abduction. Physical biological samples collected. Hybrid programs. These are what many experiencers sincerely believe. They are not established by physical evidence.

The mainstream scientific consensus: sleep paralysis and associated neurological phenomena explain the large majority of accounts. False memory formation — particularly under hypnotic regression — explains the specific narrative detail. This is the Tier 1 reading and it is well-supported.

## The John Mack Question

John Mack did not conclude that alien abduction was literally real. His actual position was more careful — and more interesting.

He concluded that something genuine was happening to his subjects. That the experiences were not pathological fabrications. That the phenomenon did not map neatly onto established psychiatric categories. He explicitly resisted both the dismissive reading and the credulous one.

Harvard initiated an investigation into whether his research violated professional standards. After a lengthy review, he was cleared. His work remains the most rigorous clinical engagement with the abduction phenomenon and is consistently misrepresented in both directions.
