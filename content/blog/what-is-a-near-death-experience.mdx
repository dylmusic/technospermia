A near-death experience is a cluster of reported psychological events occurring around the time of actual or perceived death — including a sense of profound peace, an out-of-body perspective, moving through a tunnel toward light, a life review, encounters with deceased beings, and a border that cannot or should not be crossed. These features appear with striking consistency across independent reports.

NDEs are not anecdotes. They are a studied, measurable phenomenon with validated research instruments, longitudinal studies, and an active body of peer-reviewed literature.

## What Makes NDEs Distinctive

Several features of NDEs set them apart from ordinary unusual experiences. They are consistent across cultures, causes, and individuals with no prior knowledge of the concept. They are associated with specific and lasting transformations in the people who have them — reduced fear of death, increased compassion, changed life priorities — effects that persist across long-term follow-up. And a subset of cases involves what are called veridical perceptions: reports of accurate information about the environment that the person should not have been able to access during a period of clinical death.

Each of these features has been studied. Each presents challenges for simple explanations.

## The Tier System Applied

**Tier 1 — Documented fact:** Near-death experiences are real as reported experiences — they are documented in peer-reviewed literature, occur with measurable frequency among cardiac-arrest survivors, have consistent features measurable on validated scales, and are associated with documented lasting psychological transformation. The Greyson NDE Scale is a validated research instrument. Pim van Lommel and colleagues conducted a prospective study of cardiac-arrest survivors in multiple Dutch hospitals and found roughly 18% reported NDE features. These are facts.

**Tier 2 — Suggested but not proven:** Some NDE reports include veridical perceptions — accurate reports of events or objects in the environment during periods of apparent unconsciousness. These cases exist in the literature and have been investigated, but the number of fully verified cases is small and each involves methodological questions that prevent definitive confirmation.

**Tier 3 — Speculative:** That NDEs reflect consciousness operating independent of brain activity, or that they represent genuine contact with an afterlife realm. These are interpretations of the experience; the experiences themselves are documented, but what they reveal about consciousness and death is not established.

## The Research Programs

Several serious research programs have studied NDEs in prospective, controlled ways.

Pim van Lommel and colleagues conducted a large prospective study of cardiac-arrest survivors across multiple hospitals, collecting NDE reports and follow-up data over years. The study found consistent NDE features, lasting transformation, and a subset of cases involving elements resistant to purely neurological explanation.

Sam Parnia's AWARE (AWAreness during REsuscitation) study placed visual targets on high shelves in resuscitation rooms to test whether out-of-body perceivers could accurately report them. The study found a smaller proportion of cardiac-arrest patients with awareness than expected, and a limited number of cases in which OBE claims could be tested against the hidden targets.

The University of Virginia's Division of Perceptual Studies has maintained a research program studying NDE cases alongside other survival-related phenomena for decades, documenting and investigating cases that resist standard explanations.
