If a confirmed alien signal were received, the first official step is already written down. The SETI Institute's post-detection protocols specify notification procedures, verification requirements, and coordination guidelines. The infrastructure for the beginning of a response exists.

What actually happens after that depends almost entirely on what kind of contact. And the scenarios differ so dramatically that treating them as one question misses the most important variable.

## The Four Contact Scenarios

A radio signal arriving from thousands of light-years away creates one situation — philosophical transformation, a response delay of centuries, no immediate physical threat. A probe found in our solar system creates a completely different one — definitive proof of non-human engineering, immediate technical analysis, no time buffer. Physical arrival creates another situation entirely. And the Technospermia scenario — contact already having occurred through biological seeding — implies something that has already unfolded, whether we recognize it or not.

Each scenario has been modeled, to varying degrees, by researchers who take the question seriously.

## The Tier System Applied

**Tier 1 — Documented fact:** Post-detection protocols genuinely exist in written, formal versions. The METI debate — whether to transmit active signals — is a real, ongoing scientific and policy disagreement with named proponents on both sides. Researchers across sociology, astronomy, political science, and national security have formally modeled contact scenarios. The physics of signal propagation and travel time are well understood.

**Tier 2 — Strongly suggested, unproven:** Our electromagnetic emissions have been expanding into space for long enough to have reached a significant number of nearby stellar systems. If any intelligence within that radius is monitoring, our existence is no longer a secret. The window for undetected civilization continues to close.

**Tier 3 — Speculative:** All specific contact scenarios. The Technospermia claim that contact is already past. The Dark Forest conclusion that silence is the strategically rational choice for any advanced civilization.

## The Dark Forest Problem

The Dark Forest theory — associated with physicist and novelist Liu Cixin and with independent work by other researchers — proposes that any sufficiently advanced civilization rationally maintains silence and avoids detection. The logic: in a universe with finite resources and no reliable trust mechanism between civilizations, announcing your existence to an unknown other is an existential gamble.

This reasoning has been applied seriously to the METI debate. Some researchers have argued that existing transmissions — the Voyager records, the Arecibo message, decades of radio and television — have already made the question moot. Others argue they have not reached sufficient distances to matter yet.

If the Dark Forest logic is widely applied across civilizations, the result would be exactly the silence we observe: a universe full of intelligent life, all of it listening, none of it transmitting. The paradox resolves — but the resolution is not reassuring.

## What Sociologists and Psychologists Actually Project

Research on how human populations would respond to confirmed contact generally finds that the initial reaction would be less catastrophic than popular culture suggests. Surveys consistently find that most people, including most religious believers, report they could accommodate the discovery.

The more serious concerns identified by researchers are institutional rather than psychological. Who controls the information? Who speaks for humanity in response? How do existing geopolitical rivalries play out in the race to interpret and respond? The history of how major discoveries have been managed by governments does not inspire confidence.
