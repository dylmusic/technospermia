Yes. The Pentagon has officially acknowledged multiple programs dedicated to investigating unidentified aerial phenomena. This is Tier 1 — documented, confirmed, budgeted, and in one case, legislated into existence by Congress.

The existence of the programs is not in question. What they have found, and what they have not disclosed, is where the genuine uncertainty begins.

## The Three Programs: What Is Confirmed

Three programs form the core public record. Each has been officially acknowledged, though in different ways and at different times.

The Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program operated within the Defense Intelligence Agency with funding that was not publicly acknowledged. Its existence was confirmed through investigative reporting and subsequent congressional pressure. Its director later made public claims about the program's findings that remain officially unconfirmed.

The UAP Task Force was formally established following the public release of declassified footage and growing congressional interest. It reported to the Office of the Undersecretary of Defense for Intelligence.

The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office — AARO — is the most recent and most institutionally significant. It was created by congressional statute, which means its existence and reporting requirements are legally mandated. It reports to both the Secretary of Defense and the Director of National Intelligence simultaneously.

## The Tier System Applied

**Tier 1 — Officially confirmed:** All three programs officially exist or existed. AARO is statutory. Three videos have been declassified and authenticated as genuine by the DoD. Congressional hearings have been held. Funding has been allocated. AARO has published preliminary case assessments acknowledging unexplained cases.

**Tier 2 — Credibly claimed, not officially confirmed:** Former AATIP director Luis Elizondo has stated publicly that the program concluded some UAP represent technology of non-human origin. This is a credible person with relevant professional access making a specific claim. It is not official Pentagon confirmation. David Grusch has testified under oath to the existence of a classified retrieval program operating outside the publicly acknowledged structure.

**Tier 3 — Speculative:** That the programs have made confirmed contact with non-human technology. That AARO itself is part of a deliberate managed disclosure strategy. That the full findings are being withheld in ways that go beyond standard classification for national security purposes.

## What the Released Footage Actually Shows

Three infrared videos have been officially declassified and authenticated: the Nimitz encounter footage (labeled FLIR1), GIMBAL, and GOFAST. Each shows objects displaying movement that pilots and analysts have characterized as inconsistent with known aircraft — no visible propulsion systems, no flight control surfaces, anomalous acceleration and speed profiles.

The DoD has confirmed these are authentic, unedited footage from military sensor systems. Pilots involved in the Nimitz encounter have testified publicly about what they observed with their eyes — not only on sensors.

What the footage shows, unambiguously, is objects behaving in unusual ways. What it does not establish is the origin of those objects. Authenticated anomalous footage is Tier 1. Its interpretation is Tier 3.

## The Elizondo and Grusch Claims

Luis Elizondo, former AATIP director, has stated in multiple public forums that the program concluded UAP represent technology that is not human-made. He has framed this as his own assessment informed by access he had during the program.

David Grusch, former senior UAP task force official, testified under oath to Congress that the United States operates a classified non-human craft retrieval and reverse-engineering program that has been illegally withheld from congressional oversight. He stated he has provided classified evidence to the Inspector General.

Both are credible individuals with relevant backgrounds making specific claims under circumstances — public oath, IG disclosure — that have legal consequences for false statements. Neither claim has been officially confirmed by the Department of Defense.
