Project Blue Book was the United States Air Force's formal investigation into unidentified flying object reports. It catalogued thousands of cases across nearly two decades of operation before being officially closed following an independent scientific review.

When it closed, several hundred cases remained officially classified as unidentified. No subsequent investigation resolved them. They remain part of the unexplained record.

## What It Was

The investigation was not a casual inquiry. It was a dedicated program with a formal structure, assigned staff, a standardized reporting system, and a case file database maintained over years. Military personnel and civilian witnesses submitted reports through official channels. Each case was assessed, categorized, and either explained or logged as unidentified.

The program operated across multiple phases with varying levels of rigor, different leadership approaches, and evolving methodology over its lifespan. Its files have since been largely declassified and are available for public research.

## What Blue Book Left Unresolved

The program closed the official question in a way that satisfied the institutional need to move on while leaving the intellectual question unresolved.

The several hundred cases that remained unidentified at closure were not solved, not further investigated, and not transferred to a successor program for continued analysis. They became part of the archived record — examined, classified as unexplained, and set aside.

The current era of UAP investigation represents, in part, a belated institutional acknowledgment that closing Blue Book did not resolve what Blue Book could not explain. The unexplained residual from that program is among the ancestors of the residual that contemporary investigations now officially acknowledge.
