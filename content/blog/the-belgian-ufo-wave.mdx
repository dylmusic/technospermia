Over a period of several months, thousands of witnesses across Belgium reported seeing large triangular craft with lights at the three corners and a central light below. The reports came from across the country. The Belgian Air Force investigated officially, scrambled F-16 fighters on multiple occasions, and eventually published a detailed report concluding that they could not explain the phenomenon.

That official candor — a national military acknowledging a sustained, multi-witness UAP phenomenon it could not resolve — is what distinguishes the Belgian wave from most UAP cases.

## The Scope of the Wave

Reports came from across Belgian territory over an extended period spanning two winter-season intervals. Witnesses included police officers, military personnel, and thousands of civilian observers. The descriptions were consistent: a large, silent, low-flying triangular object with a light at each apex and a large central light, sometimes accompanied by smaller lights.

Belgian police officers filed multiple official reports. Several officers described encountering the object at close range on the ground, with the craft hovering at low altitude and then departing silently.

## The Tier System Applied

**Tier 1 — Documented fact:** Thousands of witness reports were filed across Belgium over an extended period. The Belgian Air Force launched an official investigation, scrambled F-16 fighters on at least two occasions in response to reports, obtained radar contacts, and published a report acknowledging the phenomenon could not be explained. Police officers filed official reports of low-altitude triangular object sightings. The most famous photograph associated with the wave was later confessed as a fabrication by its creator.

**Tier 2 — Strongly suggested, unproven:** The consistency of descriptions across thousands of independent witnesses — a triangular shape with specific lighting configuration — suggests many witnesses were reporting observations of the same or similar objects rather than misidentifying diverse conventional phenomena.

**Tier 3 — Speculative:** That the objects were of non-human origin. That they represent a technology beyond known human capability. These conclusions are not supported by confirmed physical evidence, though they have been suggested by some researchers given the performance characteristics described.

## What the Photograph Situation Reveals

The most important lesson from the confessed photograph is about evidence standards, not about the wave itself.

The photograph was widely circulated and became associated with the Belgian case in a way that mixed fabricated evidence with genuine documented events. When it was confessed as a fake, some used this to dismiss the entire wave — including the thousands of witness reports that predate and are independent of the photograph.

This is an error of conflation in the other direction from the one the photograph was designed to create. The police reports, the F-16 scrambles, the Air Force investigation, and the thousands of civilian witness accounts did not depend on the photograph. The photograph's falsity says nothing about those.

## What Was Never Explained

The Belgian Air Force investigation concluded without an explanation for what the witnesses reported. No foreign government claimed the objects as its technology. No classified program was publicly identified as the source. The descriptions — silent, large, low-altitude, triangular — did not match any aircraft publicly acknowledged to be operating in European airspace.

The investigation was genuine. The Air Force's conclusion was genuinely inconclusive. Those two things stand regardless of the photograph.
